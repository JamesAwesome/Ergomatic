Gate 0-B · Wave F door spec §5 · PR B

The metres you rowed before you stopped

PR A made the saved row say you stopped. It still says nothing about what you did in the interval you stopped in — a rower who pulled 250 of a 500 sees a row with a dash, the same dash as the two intervals they never started. This gate is the two numbers that go in that gap, and the six decisions they need.

Frames render on top of the shipped PR A screen (merged as #276, in this tree): the close-reason line above the heroes and the History chip are already there and unchanged here. Tokens are app/src/theme/tokens.css verbatim; every frame class is copied from app/src/index.css by name with unchanged values, except the two marked NEW in the stylesheet — the pair cell and (candidate C1 only) the inline word. Frames are 372×640 portrait and 720×340 landscape, the sizes Gate 0-A and the lifecycle exit gate used, and they clip where a real phone clips. Fixtures are two seeded library workouts: Tropical Wave (TR, 5 × 500 m at 2k + 2, 26 spm, 2:00 rest — server/seed/library/tr.ts:841) and Trough (AT, 5 × 3:00 at 6k + 1, 24 spm, 1:00 rest — server/seed/library/at.ts:324). Every ratio below is computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula. Nothing is implemented.

What I am asking you to approve

Seven decisions — six you can see, plus what a screen reader says. The frames are the argument; these are the asks. Every word is a draft you can reword — the shape is what needs your eyes.

(a) The pair on a distance interval — shape and slot

Interval 3 of a 5 × 500 becomes 3  500 m  1:52.0  2k +2  250 m · 1:03  —. Two numbers, one middle dot, in the row’s own number type (mono 13px, --ink-2 — .summary-row-time’s exact declarations).

The dash stays. The pair is an extra cell in front of it, not a replacement for it — so rows 3, 4 and 5 all still end with the same mark that means this interval was never measured. That one choice is what stops a part-rowed row from reading as a rowed one, and it does the work in decision (d) too. Section 1 shows the alternative (pair replaces the dash) beside it.

Order: the pair leads with the same quantity the target is in. A 500 m target → metres first.

This one is live against code already written. There is an uncommitted draft of PR B in the worktree (git status at a50e06f3: ten modified files, none of them this gate’s) whose IntervalRow ternary renders the pair instead of the dash — the alternative in the third frame, not the recommendation. Its own comments say VALUES PENDING GATE 0-B and COPY PENDING GATE 0-B, so it is waiting on this answer by design; approving the recommendation is a one-line change to that ternary, approving the alternative is a no-op. Its class name and every type declaration already match what these frames render, arrived at independently.

(b) The pair on a time interval

A 3:00 interval’s target slot already holds a duration, so the row reads 3  3:00  1:58.0  6k +1  2:10 · 480 m  — — clock first, by the same rule: the leading number is the one you can hold against the target two cells to its left. The other number follows it, always.

The alternative — metres always first, whatever the target — is rendered beside it in section 2. It is more uniform and it puts the comparable number second on half the library.

(c) The link-lost pair: “what was last received”

When the app lost the monitor, the pair is not metres so far — it is the last reading that reached us, and the silence before the close can be arbitrarily long (spec §5.1: endSession’s linkGone includes frame silence). Three candidates in section 3.

(d) A partial that beats its own target

Task (0) allows it deliberately: a last stroke can carry 503 m past a 500 m mark before End lands, and bounding the stored number against the target would throw away a real reading. So the row must read as partial while showing a number bigger than the target. Section 4 puts it beside a measured row. The answer is (a)’s: the trailing dash, plus two empty columns where every measured row carries a bold pace and a rate.

(e) The lost banner’s zero-kept arm

Today: LOST THE MONITOR / Nothing kept. After: on kept === 0 the body drops and the title stands alone; kept ≥ 1 keeps N intervals kept. unchanged. §5 does not make this redundant — it makes it necessary: a partial is never an IntervalActual (I-B2), so a single-interval piece with a stored partial is still kept = 0, and “Nothing kept.” would now be flatly untrue on a screen that is about to show 250 m. Section 5, on the real connected surface.

(f) No split, no pace, no rate is ever derived from the pair

The pair’s clock is elapsed, not rowing time — the PM5 has no paused state, so a rower who stops pulling and then presses End banks the idle seconds too (domain/monitor/types.ts:189-191). Divide the fixture’s own numbers and you get 2:06.0 on a 1:52.0 target — a split the rower never rowed. Section 7 is that absence, rendered: the same row with and without the two columns filled.

(g) What a screen reader says — the seventh thing, and it is copy

The row’s aria-label replaces its content for assistive tech, so it is user-facing wording and belongs in this gate rather than in a task. Today an unreached row speaks Interval 4: 500 m @ 2k +2, not measured. The middle dot in the visible pair carries no meaning aloud, so the spoken form spells the two numbers out.

What the frames expose: the hero and the pair are two different authorities, and a rower can subtract

The DISTANCE hero reads 1230. The two measured rows sum to 1000. The pair says 250. 1000 + 250 = 1250, twenty metres more than the hero. Nothing here is broken and nothing is new — the hero already carried the abandoned interval’s rowed metres before this PR (storedSummary.ts’s own tier-A comment: “…so Σ steps under-counts the machine’s own machineWorkMeters by exactly that interval’s real, ROWED metres”) — but until now no screen printed a second number for those same metres, so nobody could see the two sources disagree.

They disagree because they are two sources: the hero is a total the machine or the fused close produced, the pair is our own last rowing frame before End (spec §5.1, “our number, never the machine’s”). I‑B5 says the pair must never enter a hero, which is the right call — making them agree means letting the partial into a total. I am flagging it rather than deciding it: accept the gap silently (my recommendation, and what these frames render), or PR B owes one sentence somewhere that says the headline total counts metres the rows cannot show. The fixture’s 20 m is Gate 0-A’s own approved number, carried verbatim so the two artboards stay comparable.

Row width, measured rather than guessed. At 372 px the row’s content box is 332 px, and the proposed row spends it as: index 20 · 500 m 44 · 1:52.0 52 · pace-ref 62.4 · pair 93.6 · dash 20, plus five 8 px gaps. The flexing pace-ref cell needs 36 px for 2k +2 and gets 62.4 — 26 px of headroom, nothing clipped anywhere in the frame. Taken from the rendered artboard itself (getBoundingClientRect / scrollWidth vs clientWidth on every element in every frame), not from arithmetic; the same pass is what caught C1 in section 3.

Where these frames are approximations

  • Fonts. Newsreader / Archivo / IBM Plex Mono load from Google Fonts, the three families tokens.css names. Rendering will differ by a hair from the phone’s own stack.
  • Safe areas. .screen and .connected-surface carry env(safe-area-inset-*) terms that resolve to 0 in a desktop browser. The frames use the 0 case.
  • The connected surface is the closest approximation here. Its real height is calc(100dvh - 26px) with a pane that distributes its children; these frames pin it to the artboard’s 640/340 px and drop the AVG chip beside the split target and the ELAPSED band cell. The banner itself, its type, its fill, its padding and the :has(.connected-lost) hero step-down (76/64/40 px portrait, 86/70 px landscape) are the real rules.
